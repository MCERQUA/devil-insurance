## Bundling 101: What It Actually Means

When an insurance company talks about "bundling," they mean this: buy more than one type of policy from them — typically home and auto — and they'll give you a discount on both.

The discount is real. Typical savings range from 10% to 25% depending on the carrier, your location, and your specific policies.

But bundling isn't automatically the best move. Here's why — and how to make sure you're actually saving money when you do it.

## Why Carriers Offer Bundle Discounts

Insurance companies like it when you have multiple policies with them. It reduces their customer acquisition costs, it means you're less likely to leave (fewer companies to compare against), and it gives them more data about you as a risk.

In exchange for your loyalty, they pass some of those savings back to you. That's the bundle discount.

From your perspective, it also simplifies your life: one company, one app, one call when you have a question.

## How Much Can You Actually Save?

The range is wide. Here's a realistic picture:

**Home insurance discount:** 5–15% off your homeowners premium for having auto with the same company.

**Auto insurance discount:** 5–15% off your auto premium for having home with the same company.

**Combined savings example:**
- Homeowners policy: $1,800/year → save $180–$270 with bundle
- Auto policy: $1,200/year → save $120–$180 with bundle
- Total annual savings: **$300–$450**

In Arizona, where home insurance rates have been climbing, this matters.

## The Trap: When Bundling Costs More

Here's the thing most people don't think about: bundling with the *wrong* carrier can actually cost you more money, even with the discount.

Say Carrier A offers home insurance for $1,800 and auto for $1,200. With their 15% bundle discount, you'd pay $2,550 combined.

But if Carrier B offers home for $1,400 and Carrier C offers auto for $950, you'd pay $2,350 combined — **without any bundle discount** — and get better pricing on both.

## What to Check Before Bundling

Before you commit to a bundle, here are four things to verify:

**1. Are the coverages equivalent?**
Compare apples to apples. Make sure the bundled policies have the same limits, deductibles, and key provisions as what you have now. A lower premium sometimes means a lower-quality policy.

**2. Is the base rate competitive?**
A 20% bundle discount on an already-expensive carrier might not beat a carrier that's simply 30% cheaper to begin with.

**3. Is this company good at paying claims?**
We work with carriers with strong claim-payment track records. A cheap policy that fights you at claim time isn't worth it.

**4. Are there any penalties for splitting later?**
Some carriers have provisions that increase your rates if you remove one policy from a bundle. Check this before committing.

## The Best Products to Bundle

Not all insurance types bundle equally well. Here's the rundown:

**Home + Auto:** The classic bundle. Almost every carrier offers it, discounts are reliable, and it makes administrative sense.

**Home + Auto + Umbrella:** Adding an umbrella liability policy often yields additional discounts and gives you stronger liability protection across the board.

**Auto + Renters:** If you rent rather than own, bundling your renters and auto policies works the same way and usually saves $100–$200/year.

**Home + Life:** Less common but some carriers offer it. Usually worth checking.
